# Embodied cognition and the gerund field

How Gerundelle's architecture handles embodied cognition: vision as a gerund field, space
as bonded regions, closeness as bond strength in the tournament, and the body as territory.
No tensor math, no pixel processing, no distance metrics. One architecture from perception
through understanding to action.

## The gerund field

The visual field is not pixels to be classified. It is a gerund field: every region of
what you see is already described by what it is doing. You do not see "a brown flat surface
with a cylindrical object on it." You see "supporting" under "resting." You do not see "a
red sphere moving left." You see "sliding."

The gerund is the primary perception. Color, shape, and texture are metadata on what things
are doing — secondary properties, not the thing itself. This is Chapter 1's claim (objects
are defined by their doings) applied directly to vision, and Gibson's direct perception
made computational: you see affordances, not appearances.

### Structure in the field

- **Objects emerge from gerund boundaries.** A contiguous region of "resting" is one thing.
  Where "resting" meets "supporting" is the boundary between the object and what holds it.
  The object's edge is where the gerund changes — the force boundary of Chapter 1,
  experienced as vision.
- **The 3D structure IS the gerund AST.** A box on a table: "anchoring" at the floor,
  "supporting" at the table, "resting" at the box, "locking" at the lid, "connecting" at the
  latch. The depth structure of the visual field is the same tree the force world represents
  abstractly. The AST is not a model of what you see. It is what you see.
- **Affordances are visible.** An opening in the gerund field — a place where a gerund could
  change if a force were applied — is an open slot you can see. The empty chair is a
  "supporting" region with no "resting" above it: an open slot. The invitation is perceived,
  not computed.
- **The body is in the field.** The hand is a gerund region — "extending," "gripping,"
  "empty." The arm is "reaching." The feet are "standing." The body is not separate from the
  world. It is part of the gerund field, perceived the same way.

## Space is not distance

Space between the hand and an object is not empty distance. It is gerund regions — air,
table surface, other objects — each doing something, all part of the field. The space
between hand and object is bonded gerund regions that mediate the connection between the
hand molecule and the object molecule in the tournament.

### Closeness as bond strength

As the hand moves toward the object, the gerund regions between them shrink. Fewer
intervening molecules weaken the bond between hand and object. The bond's score rises in
the tournament because the structural fit improves: less stuff in between, more direct
bonding possible.

As the hand moves away, the intervening regions grow. More molecules between hand and
object. The bond weakens. It loses tournament slots. The pull from the want dims.

"Getting closer" is not a distance metric decreasing. It is a bond strengthening in the
tournament as the gerund field between hand and object thins. "Getting farther" is a bond
weakening as the gerund field between them thickens.

## The tournament is the feedback

The mind does not need a separate feedback channel (proprioceptive sensors, binary contact
checks, distance measurements). The tournament itself is the feedback:

- **Convergence:** The bond between hand and object wins more tournament slots. The
  ownership want (the pull toward the unclaimed object) flows through the strengthening
  bond. The mind feels convergence as increasing pull — more attention drawn to completing
  the action.
- **Divergence:** The bond loses tournament slots. The pull drops. Attention shifts away
  from this action. The mind feels divergence as the pull fading — this direction is not
  working.
- **Correction:** The mind changes direction not because a gradient pointed downhill, but
  because the bond stopped winning. A different reaching direction produces a different
  gerund field change. If the bond strengthens in the new direction, the tournament keeps
  it. If not, the mind tries again.
- **Contact:** When the hand's gerund region and the object's gerund region share the same
  space in the field, the bond is direct — no intervening regions. The bond's score is at
  its maximum. The "gripping" gerund replaces "empty" in the hand's region and "resting"
  in the object's region. The want's slot funds: the object is held.

## The SOV updates through interaction

The gerund field is what you see. The SOV is what you understand about what you see. They
are not the same thing:

- You can see "connecting" at the latch without knowing that the latch keeps the lid shut.
  The gerund is perceived. The condition is learned.
- You can see "resting" on the table without knowing the object's weight, its grip, or what
  would happen if you pushed it. The gerund is given. The slots are open.

Interaction funds the slots:

1. **See the gerund field** — the raw perception of what things are doing.
2. **Open slots on what you don't understand** — causes, conditions, roles of each gerund
   region.
3. **Push something** — the strongest pull determines what.
4. **The field changes** — gerunds flip, regions merge or separate, new gerunds appear.
5. **The change funds the slots** — you now know something about what that gerund does.
6. **Funded understanding sharpens future perception** — the next gerund field you see has
   pre-funded conditions from past interactions. You see not just what things are doing but
   what they would do if pushed.

A baby sees a gerund field from birth. Everything is doing something: resting, supporting,
hanging, moving. But nothing is funded. The baby does not know why anything is doing what it
is doing. Every interaction — every push, every grasp, every drop — funds a slot. Over
months, the gerund field goes from "I see what things are doing" to "I understand what
things are doing and what would happen if I acted." Same field. Deeper funding.

## The body as primary territory

The body is the first territory and the most basic:

- **The body's region in the gerund field is held from tick 0.** The hand, the arm, the
  torso — each a gerund region the mind perceives as its own. "Standing," "breathing,"
  "extending" — these are not computed states. They are the territory the mind holds before
  it has ever touched the world.
- **An incursion on the body is contested territory.** Getting wet, feeling cold, being
  pushed — each is another force acting on the body's region. Not a negative reward signal.
  A territorial contest: something moved in my territory without my push.
- **Hunger is a depleting reserve.** The body has internal reserves (energy, warmth,
  hydration) that deplete over time, as a bridge's reserve depletes under weight. When a
  reserve crosses its threshold, a phase change: the want for food, warmth, water. Not a
  hardcoded drive. A territorial threshold crossing.
- **Pain is a contradiction.** The body's region predicted continuity (it would keep
  holding). The injury contradicts that prediction. The slot reopens with full surprise.
  Attention floods to the body's region — the same mechanism that makes a regression
  demand attention in the code mind.
- **Tool use extends the body's territory.** Chapter 2: a tool reconfigures objects to
  create an affordance the body alone could not access. In the gerund field, a tool is a
  gerund region that the hand bonds with, extending the hand's effective territory into
  regions the hand's own gerund could not reach.

## Reaching as tournament convergence: the full trace

A mind wants to pick up a cup on a table.

1. **The gerund field shows:** "supporting" (the table), "resting" (the cup), the hand's
   region ("empty," at the side of the body), intervening regions of air and table surface
   between the hand and the cup.
2. **The ownership register:** the cup is unclaimed territory. A want opens: hold the cup.
   The want's pull flows through the bond between the hand molecule and the cup molecule.
   The bond is weak — many intervening regions.
3. **The hand extends.** The gerund field changes: the hand's region moves through the
   intervening regions. Some intervening regions are now behind the hand instead of between
   hand and cup. The bond strengthens. It wins more tournament slots.
4. **The pull increases.** The mind feels the convergence. The reaching direction is
   confirmed — the bond is winning. The hand keeps extending this way.
5. **The hand touches the cup.** The intervening regions are gone. The hand's gerund region
   and the cup's gerund region share the field directly. The bond is at maximum. Contact: a
   force-state change, a receipt on the conditions slot ("reaching toward the cup produces
   contact").
6. **The hand grips.** "Empty" becomes "gripping." "Resting" becomes "held." The want's slot
   funds. The cup is territory: mine.
7. **The funded condition carries.** The next time the mind sees a gerund field with an
   unclaimed object in the hand's reachable region, the reaching condition is already funded
   from this episode. One reach, not a search.

No distance metric. No gradient. No pixel processing. The gerund field, the tournament,
the bond strength, and the ownership register — the architecture that handles fiction and
code, handling a body in a world.

## What this predicts

- **A baby's reaching improves through funding, not through motor learning curves.** The
  gerund field is perceived from birth. The conditions that link "reaching toward what I see"
  to "contact" fund over weeks of interaction. The improvement is slot funding, not weight
  adjustment.
- **Object permanence is a funded condition.** A baby who has not yet funded the condition
  "objects persist when out of sight" loses the object molecule when it leaves the gerund
  field. The Piaget finding: a baby stops reaching for a hidden object because the molecule
  left the field and the bond dissolved. The condition funds when the baby acts on a hidden
  object and it reappears — a lived receipt.
- **Tool use is a bond between body and object.** The mind bonds the hand's gerund region
  with the tool's gerund region, and the combined molecule has a larger effective territory.
  A stick in the hand extends "reaching" into regions the hand alone could not access.
  The tool is not used as a tool because it is labeled "tool." It is used because bonding
  with it extends the territory.
- **Phantom limb is a funded slot on absent territory.** The mind's funded conditions for
  the limb's gerund region persist after the limb is gone. The territory register still
  holds the region. The limb's gerund should be in the field and is not — a contradiction
  that cannot be resolved because the push that would confirm or deny is no longer
  available. The slot stays open, pulling, with no way to fund or close it.

## Connection to the existing architecture

Everything described here uses the mechanisms the book already has:

| Mechanism | In the book | In embodied cognition |

| Gerund AST | The force world's tree of doings (Ch 1) | The visual field as a 3D gerund structure |
| Open force slots | Affordances as invitations (Ch 2) | Visible openings in the gerund field |
| The tournament | Thoughts competing for slots (Ch 3) | The hand-object bond competing, strengthening as regions converge |
| Open-site tension | The earworm's non-decaying pull (Ch 4) | The want to hold the object, pulling through the bond |
| Two-phase storage | Closure and recall (Ch 5) | Motor skills as funded conditions, recalled by shape |
| The understanding register | Five typed relations per concept (Ch 7) | Conditions on what each gerund region does when pushed |
| Territorial ownership | Claims, holding, loss (Ch 8) | The body as primary territory; objects as claimable regions |
| Emotional phases | Readings of the ownership graph (Ch 9) | Frustration when the reach fails, joy when the grip takes |

No new mechanism. The gerund field is the perception layer the architecture was always
waiting for. The SOV algebra runs on it the same way it runs on the force world, the code
spec, and the chess board: a reactive world made of gerunds, funded through interaction,
understood through the tournament.
