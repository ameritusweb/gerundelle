● Here's where it lands in the running system.

  Surprise is a bid term, not something the system minimizes. A molecule's bid for a working-memory slot is:

  activation × (1 + binding_score) + open_site_tension + w_pred × forward + support

  The forward term (line 422 of molecules.py) is the molecule's current surprise level plus a novelty term that shrinks as the molecule accumulates a record of what follows
  it. So surprise makes a thought more competitive for attention — the opposite of Friston's free energy principle, where surprise is minimized. Here, surprising things get
  attended to.

  Two kinds of surprise are computed:

  1. Arrival surprise — when a molecule is perceived, its surprise is set to the information-theoretic measure -log(base_rate) / log(vocab): how unexpected its appearance is
     right now, given how often it's appeared before. This decays by 0.7 per tick (s_keep).
  2. Outcome surprise — when a learning event happens, the prediction error |outcome - mu| / 2 is stored as surprise for the molecules involved. A Dirichlet prior (1, 1, 1)
     over (good, neutral, bad) is updated by counting, and info_gain() computes the expected information gain from one more observation — a proper Bayesian calculation using
     the digamma function.

  Expected information gain is the forward-looking term: the concept map says it explicitly — "not how much surprise was reduced in the past, but how much would be reduced by
  attending now." A molecule that has been fully explored (its Dirichlet is concentrated) scores low on this term even with a great track record. This is close to active
  inference's epistemic value, but it's one factor in a competitive market, not a single quantity being optimized.

  Contradiction is the prediction-error mechanism: a stored belief predicts something; if the world answers and the prediction is wrong, the belief reopens with surprise set
  to 0.5 + 0.5 × understanding — the better understood it was, the more surprising its failure. That puts it at the top of the tournament immediately. This is the closest
  thing to predictive processing's prediction error, but it's structural: it doesn't update a generative model by gradient — it unseals a stored box, forces it back into the
  competition, and the mind re-examines it.

  How it differs from FEP specifically:

  - FEP minimizes one variational quantity. Here there's no single quantity — it's a market with many bidders and scarce slots.
  - FEP says organisms act to make predictions true (or update models to match the world). Here, surprise raises a thought's bid, so the system actively attends to what's
    unexpected, then the resulting experience updates the Dirichlet counts, which reduces future surprise naturally. The surprise reduction is a consequence of acting, not
    the objective.
  - Provenance is conserved. FEP updates a generative model indifferently to where the evidence came from. Here, lived evidence counts full, told at half, imagined at zero —
    and no operator can change that (proved in Lean).

  Where they overlap: the forward term is genuinely expected information gain from a Bayesian model, and contradiction is genuinely prediction-error-driven revision. The
  system's attention allocation by surprise, and its experiments driven by open questions (inquiry), look like active inference's epistemic foraging. But the architecture
  frames these as market phenomena (competition under scarcity with empirical feedback), not as optimization of a free energy functional.
