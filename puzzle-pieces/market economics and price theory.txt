> Where does market economics fit into the system?

● Here's where it lands in the running system.

  Market economics: scarce resources are allocated by competition among agents who bid based on what they value, with prices emerging from the bids. No central planner decides
  who gets what. The allocation arises from the competition itself, under scarcity, with feedback from outcomes.

  The concept map says it directly: "The tournament is a market. Molecules bid for scarce slots. Ownership is exclusive (one agent per slot). Bids are funded by evidence. The
  market allocates attention the way an economy allocates resources: through competition under scarcity with empirical feedback."

  The paper (gerundelle.tex, line ~187): "Cognition is a market. A mind is a marketplace whose scarce resource is commitment: at any point, a slot can be filled by one thing,
  not many. Hypotheses, patterns and wants compete for slots, and the competition is settled by evidence from acting in the world, not by enumeration, derivation or gradient."

  The mapping is structural:

  - Scarce resource: working-memory slots (Params.slots: 7 → 5 → 4). There are thousands of molecules and a handful of slots. The scarcity is what makes the allocation
    non-trivial.
  - Bidders: molecules. Each computes its bid from activation, binding score, open-site tension, expected information gain, and constraint support. The bid is the price the
    molecule pays for a slot.
  - Price discovery: the bid is not set by a central authority. Each molecule's bid is computed from its own evidence — its Dirichlet counts (how many good, neutral, and bad
    outcomes), its surprise level, its open sites. The "price" of a slot emerges from the competition.
  - Exclusive ownership: one molecule per slot. A slot held is territory. The territory register (Territory in molecules.py) tracks what the mind holds, what it has lost,
    what it contests. Ownership is the same structure for thoughts (which idea holds a slot) and for regions in the world (which agent controls a resource).
  - Feedback: outcomes update the Dirichlet counts. A molecule that wins a slot and produces a good outcome has its counts updated, its half-life extended (a_success), its
    activation boosted on re-entry. A molecule that produces bad outcomes sees its competitors' bids rise relative to its own. The market corrects itself through evidence.

  The civilization layer (SOV_CIVILIZATIONS.md) extends the same market to populations of minds:

  - Trade emerges when two populations have different funded conditions and exchange saves both search.
  - Property rights emerge when two minds contest a region and the resolution (who holds, who yields) is stored and taught.
  - Specialization: a mind whose constraint convergence in one domain is cached and transferable holds uniqueness territory — what it knows that others don't. The spice
    merchant's route is uniqueness territory; the buyer's told receipt funds at half.
  - Institutions are cached constraint convergences shared across a population. None are designed. Each is the population's convergence for a recurring coordination problem,
    cached and transmitted as told knowledge.

  The chess ladder's rung 3 (two minds on one board) is a two-agent market: White and Black each want their standing up, each bids for moves, and the outcome depends on whose
  strategy the evidence supports. White adapts to the opponent it has — against a thinking Black that doesn't recapture, White opens with Bxc8 (an even trade against the rule,
  but a won bishop against this Black). The market discovery is empirical: White learns what works against THIS opponent, not what a book says.

  How it differs from market economics specifically:

  - In economics, agents are assumed to have preferences (utility functions). Here, the bids are computed from evidence, not preferences. A molecule doesn't "want" a slot —
    its bid is high because its evidence is strong, its question is open, or its surprise is high. The allocation follows from the evidence, not from desire.
  - In economics, prices coordinate across agents. Here, the competition is within one mind — the molecules are not separate agents with separate interests, they are one
    mind's candidate thoughts. The "market" is a metaphor for the allocation mechanism, not for the social structure. (The civilization layer, where multiple minds trade, is
    where the full economic analogy holds.)
  - The paper's claim: "A market cognition algorithm is as fundamental a computational primitive as an evolutionary algorithm. An evolutionary algorithm produces solutions
    that work. A market cognition algorithm produces solutions that know WHY they work: every concluded holding carries the receipts that funded it, and every receipt names
    the world interaction that created it."
