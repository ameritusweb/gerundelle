> Where does game theory fit into the system?

● Here's where it lands in the running system.

  Game theory (von Neumann & Morgenstern, 1944; Nash, 1950): agents with competing interests make strategic choices. The outcome depends on what every agent does, not just
  one. A Nash equilibrium is a set of strategies where no agent can improve by changing only its own strategy. Signaling games (Spence, 1973): an informed agent sends a
  signal that a less-informed agent uses to decide — but the informed agent may have incentive to send a false signal.

  The fiction ladder's rung 3 is a signaling game. Mara asks Leo what keeps the lid shut. Leo knows the latch holds it shut. Leo wants the toy inside for himself. The game:
  a truthful answer would hand Mara the region (she'd undo the latch and open the box). A false answer keeps the region contested.

  The lie is not scripted. It follows from the structure of wants (fiction LADDER.md, rung 3): "A rival lies. Asked by a mind that wants what it wants (a contested want: a
  true answer would hand the region to the other), a teller Attests a counterfeit way: a push on the object that its own condition says cannot work now, one the asker has not
  tried, offered as the way to the want."

  The mechanism:
  - Leo is asked about the lid (Pose: Mara's control slot's question, carrying the hole's shape).
  - Leo's Attest checks: does the asker want what I want? The toy's region is contested (both wants open on it).
  - A truthful answer (the latch) would fund Mara's conditions slot and she'd open the box.
  - So Leo attests a counterfeit way: a push on the lid that his own condition says cannot work (gently, which he knows doesn't move it), one Mara hasn't tried.
  - Mara acts on it (told receipt, funded at half, believed by the planner at the mind's credulity).
  - It fails. The told receipt is retracted. The teller's reliability record is marked down. Anger forms.

  The motive is structural, not a character trait: "A speaker lies exactly when the asker wants what the speaker wants and a true answer would hand the region over. A speaker
  with no competing want, holding the same knowledge, tells the truth." Rung 2's Ben, holding the same knowledge about the latch, told the truth — because his want was
  closed. The only difference is the want structure.

  Rung 4 is a repeated game. Mara and Leo act in the same world, alternating. Leo's want is the opposite of Mara's (keep the box shut). The equilibrium is not a Nash
  equilibrium — it's a threshold crossing. Each time one undoes the other's work, the other's resolve drains. The one who hits zero first gives up. The "equilibrium" is
  whichever mind outlasts the other, and it depends on reserves and stakes, not on strategic optimization. Doubling Leo's reserve flips the ending (fiction LADDER.md, rung 4:
  lean-stubborn).

  The chess ladder's rung 3 (two minds on one board, DuelWorld) is the full two-agent game. Both White and Black are Gerundelle minds. Each wants its standing up. Each adapts
  to the opponent it has:
  - The thinking Black rarely takes back (34 times vs the rule's 624). After a capture it moves its knight away or plays something else.
  - White adapts: in the hanging configuration it mostly opens Bxc8 (an even trade against the rule, a won bishop against this Black), not the combination it learned against
    the rule.
  - Black meets its want only on White's mistakes (89 times in the base arm, 35 in the full arm where White makes fewer).

  How it differs from game theory specifically:

  - Game theory assumes agents maximize a utility function by choosing strategies. Here, agents don't choose strategies — they learn conditions from experience, set sub-wants,
    and act on what their evidence supports. The "strategy" is the accumulated constraint convergence, not a plan chosen from a strategy set.
  - Nash equilibrium assumes rationality and common knowledge. Here, each mind knows only what it has experienced. Leo doesn't compute Mara's optimal strategy — he lies
    because his wants and hers conflict and a truthful answer would hand the region over. Mara doesn't compute Leo's incentive — she acts on what she's told, retracted when
    the world contradicts it.
  - Game theory's signaling games require a signaling cost (Spence's education signal) for honest signaling to survive. Here, dishonesty is detected by the world's feedback
    (the lie fails, the told receipt is retracted, the teller's record is marked down) and by the asker's own discovery (the real condition, found by contrast, exposes the
    lie). Trust is a computed quantity: (confirmed + KAPPA/2) / (told + KAPPA), updated by each interaction. There is no signaling cost, only empirical verification.
  - The repeated game (rung 4) has no discount factor or tit-for-tat strategy. The dynamics are resolve draining under repeated undoing, and the outcome is a threshold
    crossing. The game-theoretic equilibrium concept doesn't apply — the ending is determined by reserves, not by strategic reasoning.
