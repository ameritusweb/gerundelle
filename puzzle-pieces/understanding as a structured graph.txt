> Where does the idea of understanding as a structured graph with typed relations, filled slots, and unknowns fit into the system?

● Here's where it lands in the running system.

  The idea: understanding isn't a number or a weight — it's a graph. Each concept is a node. Each node has typed relation slots (what causes it, what it causes, what
  co-occurs with it, what role it plays, what conditions it). Some slots are filled (funded by evidence). Some are open (unknowns). The shape of what's open tells the mind
  what kind of evidence it needs. The graph is the understanding, and it's inspectable: you can look at any concept and see exactly what's known, what's unknown, and where the
  evidence came from.

  This is the Understanding register (class Understanding in molecules.py, line ~1634). Every concept the mind encounters gets an entry in the register. Each entry holds typed
  relations, each with receipts by provenance:

  Understanding.RELS = ("causes", "caused_by", "co_occurs", "role", "conditions")

  Each relation slot for a concept holds a list of (other_concept, [seen, told, worked]) triples. The funding of a slot is computed with provenance weights
  (Understanding.funding): lived and worked-out receipts at full weight, told at the mind's credulity (Params.credulity, default 0.5). A concept's level of understanding
  (Understanding.level) is the mean over relation types of their funding, capped at sov_m0 each, as a share of sov_m0.

  The open slots are the unknowns (Understanding.open_slots): relation types whose funding is below 1.0. An open slot has a shape — it's a typed question ("what causes this?"
  or "what conditions this?"). The concept map says it: "An open slot surrounded by known bonds has a shape — it pulls for what would fill it, and the shape tells the mind
  what kind of evidence it needs. Remove the bonds and the unknown vanishes."

  The unknowns pull. An open slot on a concept the mind is attending to generates an SOV molecule (a question) that enters the tournament and competes for attention in its own
  track (Params.sov_slots: 3 → 2 → 2). An SOV molecule binds only to knowns the concept has evidence with in the raw records (co-wins, what followed what in the experience
  records). When it wins together with a known, that relation is written back to the register. At the top, an SOV molecule poses a question for each open slot; a question
  closes when the register fills its slot. The concept map: "Why uncertainty or a lack of understanding should generate thought molecules. An open slot with unfilled binding
  sites adds a constant, non-decaying tension to the molecule's bid every tick until the site is filled. The incomplete thought keeps returning to consciousness not because
  it's surprising but because it's unfinished. That's the earworm mechanism."

  Relations are written in two ways:

  1. Directly, from what the pool already discovered: a split's boundary conditions the split thought (a contrast reveals what matters); a recognition's clause conditions an
     action's goal; a filler has a role in what it filled; a part has a role in its composite; an explanation heard with its keys gives told co-occurrences.
  2. By working it out: a concept in play (an instance wins the top while the concept is not fully understood) is cued into the pool as an SOV molecule whose sites are its
     open slots. It binds to knowns it has evidence with, and when it wins together with one, that relation is written.

  How understanding is used:

  - Inspectability: Understanding.relations(c) returns every filled relation for a concept. The concept map: "Every slot has receipts. Every receipt has provenance. Every
    condition has the failures that revealed it. Expertise is a cached constraint convergence, and the cache is legible."
  - Incremental update: "A contradiction reopens the one slot it contradicted, not the whole web. Everything not contradicted stays funded." A version update, a rule change, a
    new discovery — each reopens locally and the rest holds.
  - Complexity as depth: "Complexity is bonding depth, not an atomic bottom layer. A half-formed thought is a molecule with open binding sites. A complex thought is fragments
    bonded to fragments bonded to fragments. There is no simplest; there is only shallower and deeper."
  - The learning curve: a concept with all slots filled is fully understood (level 1.0). The mind stops searching for evidence about it. A concept with many open slots pulls
    hard for attention. The cost of search is proportional to the open slots — the mind's ignorance — not to the size of the problem.

  The 15 relation forms (RELATION_TYPES.md) extend the five register types into the theory's full vocabulary: identity/type-of, part/whole, relation-to, duration, motion,
  possession transfer, status transfer, transformation, exchange, communication, opposition, competition, cause, enable, prevent — each decomposed into a primitive (BE, STAY,
  GO, force, CAUSE, LET) and a field (identificational, compositional, relational, temporal, spatial, possessional, social, mental). The register's five types are what the
  code measures; the 15 forms are what the theory says understanding consists of.

  How it differs from knowledge graphs and semantic networks:

  - A knowledge graph (Sowa's conceptual graphs, RDF, OWL) is a static database: nodes and edges, with types, filled by assertion or extraction. Here, the graph is grown by
    the mind's own experience: each relation is funded by specific evidence (seen, told, worked out), and funding determines how well understood the concept is. An unfunded
    relation doesn't exist in the graph — it's an open slot, not a missing edge.
  - Minsky's frames have typed slots with defaults. Here, there are no defaults — an unfilled slot is an unknown, and it pulls for evidence. The pull is structural (open-site
    tension, constant, non-decaying), not a missing value filled by a best guess.
  - A semantic network doesn't distinguish provenance. Here, every receipt carries where it came from: the same relation "A causes B" has different standing if it was lived
    (full weight), told (half weight), or worked out from evidence (full weight). The Lean proof guarantees that told can never become lived without a Fit event.
  - The graph is not read-only. Contradiction reopens a slot. A split refines a concept into two halves. Unification merges halves back. The graph is a living structure that
    tracks the mind's understanding as it grows, retracts, and restructures.
