> Where do Kuhn's paradigm shifts fit into the system?

● Here's where it lands in the running system.

  Kuhn's Structure of Scientific Revolutions (1962): science doesn't progress by steady accumulation. It alternates between normal science (puzzle-solving within a paradigm)
  and revolutionary science (the paradigm itself is overthrown). A paradigm shift happens when anomalies — observations the paradigm can't explain — accumulate to the point
  where the paradigm is abandoned for a new one. The shift is not gradual; it's a gestalt switch. The old paradigm is not refuted by one experiment; it's abandoned when the
  weight of anomalies becomes intolerable.

  Contradiction and reopening is the mechanism. Mind.contradict (molecules.py, line ~1013): a stored belief (a box) predicts something. If the world answers and the prediction
  is wrong, the box reopens with surprise set to 0.5 + 0.5 × understanding. The better understood the belief was — the more it was a settled paradigm — the more surprising
  its failure.

  The concept map: "A stored belief whose prediction fails reopens with full surprise, forcing revision. The mind can't ignore a failed prediction — the surprise injection
  puts the contradicted belief at the top of the tournament. Not a rule. A structural consequence of the reopen mechanism."

  The Kuhnian structure maps onto the system's phases:

  Normal science is the settled state. A molecule with no open sites, that has won the top for three consecutive ticks with flat precision, precipitates into storage (settled
  closure). Its predictions hold. Its keys cool slowly. The mind doesn't revisit it — it's understood, it works, it's the paradigm. Evidence accumulates in the Dirichlet
  counts. Understanding grows. The learning curve (concept map): "A mind with a fully cached convergence doesn't search: the resonance fires from storage in one tick." Normal
  science is the cached convergence doing its work.

  An anomaly is a failed prediction. The box predicted something; the world contradicted it. But one anomaly doesn't overthrow the paradigm. The surprise injection
  (0.5 + 0.5 × understanding) puts the contradicted belief at the top of the tournament — it's conscious again, its sites reopened — but if the mind's further investigation
  confirms the original understanding, the belief re-settles. A single anomaly forces examination; it doesn't force revolution.

  A paradigm shift is a split. When a molecule's outcomes are bimodal — good in some situations, bad in others (the anomalies are systematic, not random) — the conflation
  detector fires (Meta: "conflated"). The meta layer splits the concept on a boundary condition: half the outcomes with the condition, half without. If the halves are unimodal
  and opposite, the split is confirmed. The old concept is replaced by two: one where the old paradigm holds, and one where it doesn't. The boundary condition is the new
  paradigm's core insight — the thing the old paradigm couldn't see because it treated both cases as one.

  The fiction ladder's rung 1 showed this cycle. Tom pushes the crate, it moves (normal science: his push moves the crate). He pushes again, it doesn't move (anomaly:
  surprise, the belief reopens). More pushes, more anomalies — but the successes and failures are systematic: it moves when the flag swings, it doesn't when the flag is
  still. The conflation detector fires. The concept "push the crate" splits on the boundary "the flag swinging." Two paradigms: pushing-with-wind (works) and
  pushing-without-wind (doesn't). "The crate moved only if the flag swung."

  The chess ladder showed it at a different scale. Rung 1: the mind learns "Rxd5 wins material" (normal science). In the defended configuration, Rxd5 loses the exchange
  (anomaly). The contrast reveals the boundary: the knight defended by the bishop. The old concept splits into "Rxd5 with the knight hanging" (wins) and "Rxd5 with the knight
  attacked" (loses). Each paradigm is valid in its own domain.

  How it differs from Kuhn specifically:

  - Kuhn's paradigm shifts are social: a community of scientists adopts a new paradigm. Here, the shift is within one mind — one molecule's outcomes split on a boundary
    condition. The social version would be the civilization layer (SOV_CIVILIZATIONS.md): a population whose cached convergence is contradicted by a new world. Not yet run
    at this scale.
  - Kuhn argues that paradigms are incommensurable — scientists in different paradigms can't fully communicate because the same terms mean different things. Here, the two
    halves of a split share everything but the boundary condition. The split preserves the structure (the gerund skeleton) and adds one distinction. They are commensurable by
    construction.
  - Kuhn's revolutions are rare, dramatic events. Here, splits happen whenever the conflation detector fires — they're routine cognitive operations. A mind that encounters a
    bimodal outcome splits it. The frequency depends on how often the world is more complex than the mind's current concepts — which is most of the time at the start (many
    splits), and rarely once understanding is deep (few splits). The revolutionary/normal distinction is a matter of degree, not kind.
  - Kuhn says nothing about the weight of the contradiction. Here, surprise = 0.5 + 0.5 × understanding: a well-understood belief contradicted gets MORE surprise than a
    poorly understood one. The paradigm shift is proportionally more disruptive the more the old paradigm was settled. This matches Kuhn's intuition but gives it a number.
