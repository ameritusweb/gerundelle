> Where does the "thoughts as molecules" idea fit into the system?

● Here's where it lands in the running system.

  The idea (from what_ifs.txt): thoughts behave as molecules — they compete and bind with one another, support each other, and winning thoughts compete at the next level of
  the tournament. There are multiple levels. The winners are the ones that get conscious attention. Some of the thoughts are meta-thoughts exhibiting meta-cognition.

  This is the core of molecules.py — the tournament IS this idea, implemented literally. A molecule (dataclass Mol) has: an id, activation (act), tags, open sites (sites),
  bond endpoints (bond), parts, and strength. The Mind class runs the tournament: pool of molecules, scarce slots per level (Params.slots: 7 → 5 → 4), and one tick of
  thinking (Mind.think) that resolves bindings, computes bids, and selects winners at each level.

  The molecular behaviors described in what_ifs.txt map one to one:

  - Compete: molecules bid for scarce slots. The bid is activation × (1 + binding_score) + open_site_tension + w_pred × forward + support. Molecules that don't win a slot
    decay by half-life. The competition is the mechanism of attention: what wins is what gets attended to.
  - Bind: a molecule resolves to its best partner among the competitors at each level (Mind.resolve). A bond is a recognition molecule with two binding sites (Mol.bond),
    holding two thoughts together. It can act silently as a structural edge (boosting both ends' bids) or, when examined, become a conscious thought in its own right — the
    same piece of knowledge in two roles, resolved by the tournament.
  - Support each other: open-site tension (Params.T) pulls a molecule toward whatever fills its sites, and a filler toward whatever it fills. Constraint support
    (Mind.support, Params.support = "candidates") adds the bid of every constraint molecule the move could clear, in superposition.
  - Winning thoughts compete at the next level: each level's winners are the competitors for the next level (Mind.think, line ~539: "for L, k in enumerate(self.p.slots)").
    The levels narrow: 7 → 5 → 4. The top level is conscious attention.
  - Dissolve: a molecule whose activation falls below Params.floor is swept from the pool (_sweep). Not deleted from the experience records — just too quiet to compete.
    Cooling by half-life, modulated by five factors: success, surprise, novelty, blocked momentum, closure.

  The tournament of levels described in what_ifs.txt:

  - Level 1 (seed stage): percepts enter the pool with activation 1.0 (Mind.present). A flood of raw molecules, some dissipating almost instantly if they lack relevance.
  - Level 2 (selection): molecules compete based on their bids at level 0. Emotional salience (phase molecules anchored on territory slots), cognitive strength (binding
    score), logical coherence (the force between related molecules), novelty (the forward-looking term).
  - Level 3 (consolidation / conscious attention): the top level's winners. A molecule that wins here gets its experience records updated, bonds reinforced, open sites
    addressed. A molecule with no open sites that has won the top for three consecutive ticks with flat precision precipitates into storage (Mind.settled, Params.dwell) — a
    memory is formed.
  - Action: MCTS guided by the top-level winners (mcts in molecules.py). The plan is read off the mind's own model, not searched exhaustively.

  Meta-thoughts are implemented as the Meta class (molecules.py). A meta molecule is a thought about thinking: "surprised" (a molecule's outcome was unexpected), "torn" (two
  bindings are close), "wrong" (a prediction failed), "resolved" (an open question was answered). Meta molecules compete in their own track (Params.meta_slots: 3 → 2 → 2),
  with their own slots, so they don't crowd out regular thoughts and regular thoughts don't crowd them out. Meta molecules that have an operation (Meta.OPERATES) get priority
  for their slots. A meta molecule binds only to its subject (the thought it's about), and its predictions are scored: a "surprised" molecule predicts the subject's next
  binding will differ, and if it does, the subject's half-life is extended. Meta-cognition gates the operations: inquiry (testing a hypothesis) fires only when its meta
  molecule wins a slot.

  The concept map: "Why not all thoughts should be in constant focus. The tournament has scarce slots (7 → 5 → 4). Thousands of molecules compete; a handful win. What doesn't
  win fades. Attention is a resource allocated by competition, not a spotlight the mind points."
