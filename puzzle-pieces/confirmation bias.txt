> Where does confirmation bias fit into the system?

● Here's where it lands in the running system.

  Confirmation bias (Wason, 1960; Nickerson, 1998): the tendency to search for, interpret, and recall information that confirms one's preexisting beliefs, while ignoring or
  discounting information that contradicts them. People test their hypotheses by looking for confirming evidence rather than disconfirming evidence. The bias is not a design
  flaw in any one system — it appears robustly across tasks, ages, and cultures.

  The self-confirming sub-want loop is the architecture's own version, found empirically three times in the chess ladder, each time as a failure first:

  1. Rung 1c, seed 5: the mind learned "Re8 mates when the f7 pawn is attacked" (a coincidental correlation from the first contrast). It set the sub-want "f7 attacked" and
     met it with Rd7, 326 times. Every episode. The condition was never tested without the behavior because the behavior always created the condition. Contradiction had
     nothing to catch: the move never failed in the context it created.

  2. Rung 1, seed 6 (after the label fix): learned "Rxd5 wins when my bishop on a6 is hanging." Lost the exchange 43 times making its bishop hang — because in the
     undefended configuration (where Rxd5 wins anyway), the bishop could be made to hang via Bb7, and Rxd5 always won there. Self-confirmation: the belief held 52 of 52
     times where it was tested, because the test that would refute it (Rxd5 without the bishop hanging) was preempted by the sub-want always making the bishop hang first.

  3. Rung 1b, Stockfish seed 2: learned "Bxc8 needs my bishop hanging." Lost 37 exchanges acting on it.

  The concept map's section on contradiction: "A stored belief whose prediction fails reopens with full surprise, forcing revision. The mind can't ignore a failed prediction."
  But here the belief never fails — the mind's own actions create the condition it expects every time. The confirmation bias is not a filter on incoming evidence. It's a
  structural loop: the hypothesis generates the actions that generate the evidence that confirms the hypothesis.

  The traced diagnosis (chess LADDER.md, rung 1c): "The belief was never wrong where it was tested. The test that refutes it was playable, but its sub-want always made the
  condition hold first: self-confirmation, so contradiction had nothing to catch."

  The architecture's fixes, each discovered as a principle from the failure:

  1. sub_tested (Params.sub_tested): what a way needs (needs) counts a split's condition only where the split's other half holds records — a condition never seen absent is not
     known to matter. The trace: "material down" was a sub-want from a nested split whose other half was empty; every record of Rd8 with e8 held also had material down,
     because the only way e8 became held was by sacrificing a rook. A condition the mind has never observed absent is not funded as a requirement.

  2. sub_cost (Params.sub_cost): while a sub-want is served, a push whose outcome moved the root want's object away from the root want is no means to it. A means that costs
     what it's supposed to help is blocked.

  3. Weight by receipts (rung 1d): each constraint's weight is 1 - 1/2^n for the n failures of its kind that carried it. A constraint seen once weighs half. This means a
     coincidental constraint from one contrast has less influence than one confirmed by many failures. The worst case fell from 37 to 25 attempts.

  The fiction ladder's rung 1 found confirmation bias in story form: "Tom thought his push had moved the crate" — a success with no known condition credited to the push
  (a worked receipt: believe). A later failure contradicts it (surprise, belief retracted). The fix: a success whose condition is unknown is believed provisionally; a single
  failure retracts it and the contrast teaches the real condition. The architecture CAN be wrong, but it doesn't stay wrong once the world contradicts it — unless the
  self-confirming loop prevents the contradiction from ever occurring.

  How it differs from confirmation bias in the literature specifically:

  - Wason's confirmation bias is about how people test hypotheses: they seek confirming cases and neglect disconfirming ones. Here, the bias is not about which evidence the
    mind seeks — the Inquiry mechanism suggests experiments that test conditions, including disconfirming ones. The bias is about which evidence the mind can encounter: the
    sub-want's actions create the condition before the test runs, so the disconfirming case never arises.
  - Nickerson's review describes confirmation bias as a pervasive cognitive tendency. Here, it's a specific structural failure with specific structural fixes. The bias is not
    in the mind's interpretation of evidence (the Dirichlet counts are updated honestly) but in the mind's actions shaping which evidence is available. The fix is in the
    action selection (sub_tested, sub_cost, weight by receipts), not in the evidence evaluation.
  - The architecture demonstrates that an honest evidence processor — one that updates its beliefs correctly from every datum it receives — can still exhibit confirmation
    bias if its actions are controlled by its beliefs. The bias is in the loop, not in the processing.
