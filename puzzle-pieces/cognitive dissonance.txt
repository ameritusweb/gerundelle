> Where does cognitive dissonance (Festinger) fit into the system?

● Here's where it lands in the running system.

  Cognitive dissonance (Festinger, 1957): holding two contradictory beliefs at once produces psychological discomfort. The discomfort motivates the person to resolve the
  conflict — by changing one belief, adding a rationalizing belief, or minimizing the importance of the conflict. The dissonance is not the contradiction itself; it's the
  discomfort of holding both sides at once before resolving them.

  The conflation detector is the mechanism. Meta.observe in molecules.py: when a molecule's outcomes are bimodal — good outcomes AND bad outcomes, both above 25% of its trials
  — the meta layer emits "conflated." A conflated molecule is one the mind is treating as one thing when it's actually two: pushing the crate sometimes works and sometimes
  doesn't, and the mind hasn't yet distinguished the cases.

  The concept map: "When a thought's outcomes are bimodal (good and bad both above 25%), the conflation detector fires and splits it on a boundary condition. If both halves
  stay unimodal and opposite, the split is confirmed. If both halves behave the same, the split is collapsed and another boundary is tried."

  The dissonance is structural: the molecule holds two contradictory records (some trials say good, some say bad) and both are real. The discomfort maps onto the meta
  molecule's bid: a "conflated" meta molecule competes for the meta track's slots, and if it wins, its operation fires — differentiate the concept. The operation is the
  resolution: split the molecule on the boundary condition that separates the good outcomes from the bad.

  The chess ladder showed this at rung 1. The mind pushes Rxd5: sometimes it wins material (the knight was hanging), sometimes it loses the exchange (the knight was defended).
  Both outcomes are real. The conflation detector fires. The split: "Rxd5 with the knight hanging" (good) and "Rxd5 with the knight attacked" (bad). Two concepts where there
  was one. The dissonance is resolved by distinguishing the cases.

  The fiction ladder's rung 1 showed it in story form. Tom pushes the crate: "He pushed the crate hard. The crate slid toward the shed. Tom thought his push had moved the
  crate. Then he pushed the crate hard again. But this time the crate did not move." The same push, different outcomes. The dissonance (he believed his push worked, now it
  doesn't) is resolved by the split: "The crate moved only if the flag swung."

  Festinger's three resolution strategies:

  1. Change one belief: the split changes the molecule. What was one concept ("push the crate") becomes two ("push with wind" and "push without wind"). The mind doesn't keep
     the old belief alongside the new one — the split replaces it.

  2. Add a rationalizing belief: the architecture doesn't do this directly. But the self-confirming sub-want loop (chess rung 1c) is the pathological version: the mind holds
     a coincidental condition, acts on it, the action creates the condition it expects, and contradiction has nothing to catch. The "rationalization" is the loop's
     self-confirmation — the mind never encounters evidence against its belief because its own actions prevent the test.

  3. Minimize importance: parking (Meta.operate for "stuck"): a molecule blocked too long with too many open sites is parked — removed from competition, keyed for later. This
     doesn't resolve the dissonance; it defers it. The molecule returns when circumstances change (resonance reopens it). Parking is the closest mechanism to "I'll stop
     thinking about this."

  The "torn" meta molecule is the felt dissonance before the split. Meta.observe: when a molecule's top binding is nearly tied with the runner-up (within 5%), "torn" emits.
  The mind is pulled in two directions by the same thought. The torn molecule's prediction: the top binding will flip (the dissonance will make the mind vacillate). If
  confirmed — the binding does flip — the torn molecule persists, keeping the conflict in the meta track's awareness.

  How it differs from Festinger specifically:

  - Festinger's dissonance is a drive — an aversive state that motivates resolution. Here, the conflation is a detected state with a specific operation (differentiate). The
    "motivation to resolve" is the meta molecule's bid for the meta track's slots: if the conflated molecule wins, the split is attempted. If it doesn't win (other meta
    observations are more pressing), the dissonance persists but doesn't force resolution.
  - Festinger's theory applies to beliefs held consciously. Here, the conflation detector operates on outcome distributions — it fires whether or not the molecule is at the
    top. A molecule can have bimodal outcomes at level 0 without ever reaching consciousness. The detection is statistical, not introspective.
  - Festinger's resolution can involve self-deception (rationalizing to reduce discomfort). Here, the split is honest: it's determined by the actual boundary condition in the
    data (the one fact that separates the good outcomes from the bad). If no boundary exists (the bimodality is noise, not a real distinction), the split fails and is
    collapsed (Meta: "same" operation, unify). The architecture can't rationalize — it can only split on evidence or fail to split.
  - The self-confirming loop (rung 1c) is the closest to rationalization, but it's a failure mode the architecture detects and corrects (sub_tested: a condition never seen
    absent is not known to matter), not a feature.
