> Where does process philosophy (Whitehead) fit into the system?

● Here's where it lands in the running system.

  Whitehead's process philosophy (Process and Reality, 1929): the world is not made of things with properties. It is made of processes — "actual occasions of experience" that
  are happenings, not substances. An entity is what it is doing, not what it is made of. Reality is a web of becomings, not a collection of beings.

  The world model is a direct implementation. The paper says it (gerundelle.tex, line ~182): "The ontology is Whitehead's: things are what they are doing." In force_world.py,
  every object is described by a gerund — a doing. A jar is "charged" (it holds potential drawn from a spring). A boulder is "resting" (its weight balances the ground's
  support). A lid is "fastened" (the latch's grip exceeds the spring's push). There are no coordinates, no distances, no materials. Each object is a balance of external
  forces against internal reserves (grip, capacity, seal), and when a force crosses a reserve the gerund flips: charged becomes empty, resting becomes falling, fastened
  becomes free.

  The concept map (center-left column): "Gerund ASTs: every node is a gerund with typed force slots, not a noun with properties. A stone arch and a steel beam share one
  skeleton. Material doesn't appear in the representation; only what things are doing under forces."

  The concept map (right column): "Gerunds name what things are doing, not what they are made of. A stone arch and a steel beam both 'support.' Material invariance means one
  vocabulary covers every domain. The 102-gerund set was chosen to close: a small number of primitives covering the space."

  This is what makes the architecture domain-independent. The fiction ladder's rung 6 showed recall by shape: a mind that learned a latch holds a box shut transfers that
  structure to a bolt holding a cupboard shut — the gerund skeleton matches (a fastening holding a container), even though the objects are different. "The company moved to
  London" and "the family emigrated to Canada" share one skeleton. strip() removes names from a gerund tree, leaving typed slots where names were: two programs doing the same
  operations on the same shape of data flow land on the same skeleton, whatever their variable names or domain.

  How it differs from Whitehead specifically:

  - Whitehead's process philosophy is metaphysical — it describes ultimate reality. Here it's computational: the gerund representation is a data structure (an AST with typed
    force slots), and its consequences are measured (recall by shape transfers across domains, material invariance lets one mind run in story, code, chess, and civilization
    worlds).
  - Whitehead's "actual occasions" are momentary and perish. Here, a gerund persists as long as the force balance holds — "resting" continues until the support gives way.
    The flip (resting → falling) is the event; the gerund between flips is ongoing.
  - Whitehead's process philosophy has no mechanism for composition. Here, gerund ASTs nest: narrating contains explaining contains describing. The nesting with names
    stripped is the skeleton. Composition combines a header from one seed with a growth from another (cognitivecode rung 7), producing programs search never found. The
    generative power is combinatorial: a small set of gerunds and a nesting rule produce a space of structures large enough to cover both natural language and code.
  - The vocabulary of forces and reserves follows Talmy's force dynamics (Talmy 1988) — an excavation of how people already represent causation in language. The 15 relation
    forms (RELATION_TYPES.md) are laid out by primitive and field (after Jackendoff). So the system takes Whitehead's ontology ("things are doings"), Talmy's dynamics ("doings
    are force balances"), and Jackendoff's primitives ("force balances decompose into BE, STAY, GO, CAUSE, LET") and implements all three.
