> Where does Piaget's accommodation and assimilation fit into the system?

● Here's where it lands in the running system.

  Piaget's genetic epistemology (1952, 1970): a child builds understanding through two complementary processes. Assimilation: fitting new experience into existing schemas
  (the world conforms to what you already know). Accommodation: changing your schemas when new experience doesn't fit (your knowledge conforms to the world). Equilibrium
  is the balance between them; disequilibrium — a mismatch between schema and experience — drives learning.

  Both are implemented. The distinction maps onto two different code paths in the same architecture:

  Assimilation is confirmation. A molecule wins the tournament, the mind acts on it, and the outcome matches what the Dirichlet prior predicted. The experience record is
  updated (a count added to the good, neutral, or bad bucket), the molecule's precision improves, and its half-life is extended by the a_success modulator. The schema absorbed
  the new experience. Nothing restructured. This is what happens on most ticks when the mind is acting in a well-understood domain.

  Accommodation is contradiction. A stored belief (a box in Mind.boxes) predicts something: whenever its "when" tags appear among the winners, it expects its "expect" tag in
  the world's answer. If the world answers and the expectation is wrong, Mind.contradict fires: the box reopens with surprise set to 0.5 + 0.5 × understanding. The better
  understood the belief was, the more surprising its failure — the more violent the disequilibrium. The reopened molecule re-enters the pool with its original sites (its open
  questions), competing again, forced to be revised by new evidence.

  The concept map: "A stored belief whose prediction fails reopens with full surprise, forcing revision. The mind can't ignore a failed prediction — the surprise injection
  puts the contradicted belief at the top of the tournament. Not a rule. A structural consequence of the reopen mechanism."

  The fiction ladder's rung 1 showed the full cycle: Tom pushes the crate, it moves (assimilation: his belief "my push moves the crate" is confirmed). Later he pushes and
  it doesn't move (disequilibrium: the prediction fails). The belief is contradicted. The surprise injection forces it into the competition. The contrast between the success
  and the failure reveals the condition: the crate moved only when the flag was swinging (a hidden driver, the wind). The schema accommodates: "my push moves the crate when
  the wind blows." The belief and retraction arc: "Tom thought his push had moved the crate... But this time the crate did not move... The crate moved only if the flag swung."

  Piaget's object permanence is in the embodied cognition architecture (EMBODIED_COGNITION.md): "A baby who has not yet funded the condition 'objects persist when out of sight'
  loses the object molecule when it leaves the gerund field. The Piaget finding: a baby stops reaching for a hidden object because the molecule left the field and the bond
  dissolved. The condition funds when the baby acts on a hidden object and it reappears — a lived receipt."

  The distinction (Meta class in molecules.py) refines accommodation further. When a molecule's outcomes are bimodal — good in some situations, bad in others — the conflation
  detector fires. The meta layer splits the concept on a boundary condition: half the outcomes with the condition, half without. If the halves are unimodal and opposite, the
  split is confirmed. If they behave the same, the split is collapsed and another boundary is tried. The concept map: "When a thought's outcomes are bimodal (good and bad both
  above 25%), the conflation detector fires and splits it on a boundary condition." This is accommodation with a mechanism: the schema doesn't just change — it splits into two
  schemas, each valid in its own context, the boundary learned from evidence.

  How it differs from Piaget specifically:

  - Piaget's schemas are described in terms of actions and operations but have no formal structure. Here, the schema is the understanding register: typed relation slots, each
    with provenance-weighted funding, open slots as unknowns with measurable pull. Accommodation is a specific code path (contradict, split, reopen) with measurable
    consequences (the surprise level, the number of reopened sites, the learning that follows).
  - Piaget's equilibrium is a tendency. Here, it's a measurable state: a concept with all slots funded, whose predictions hold, is in equilibrium. A failed prediction is
    quantified (surprise = 0.5 + 0.5 × understanding). The disequilibrium has a magnitude.
  - Piaget's stages (sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete, formal) are age-gated developmental periods. Here, the development is a ladder: each rung adds a capability
    (conditions, sub-wants, constraint convergence, told knowledge, social exchange), and the transition is measured (how many tries, which conditions learned, what the mind
    does with what it knows). The stages are capabilities, not ages, and a mind can be at different stages for different concepts.
  - Piaget doesn't distinguish provenance. Here, a schema funded by lived experience (full weight) is structurally different from one funded by told experience (half weight),
    even if they contain the same knowledge. Accommodation from a lived contradiction is immediate and high-surprise. Accommodation from a told contradiction (someone tells
    you your belief is wrong) is at the teller's reliability, not at full weight.
