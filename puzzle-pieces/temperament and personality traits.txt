> Where does personality / temperament theory (Big Five, etc.) fit into the system?

● Here's where it lands in the running system.

  The Big Five (Costa & McCrae, 1992): personality is described by five broad traits — openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism. Each person sits
  at a point in this five-dimensional space. Traits are stable, heritable, and predict behavior across situations.

  The temperament system (temperament.py, described in fiction LADDER.md) gives each character a Temperament — a set of parameters that, varied, produce different nameable
  characters from the same algebra. The rule for what belongs in temperament: "every value must still give a working mind whose story round-trips, and different values must
  give different, nameable characters."

  The five temperament parameters:

  | trait        | SOV quantity                                                      | Params               |
  | patience     | failures running on a wanted region before the want is parked     | Params.patience      |
  | persistence  | how slowly a repeated question loses its pull, q / (q + n)        | Params.persistence   |
  | curiosity    | the pull of unknowns no want pays for                             | Params.curiosity     |
  | credulity    | the weight of told receipts                                       | Params.credulity     |
  | stubbornness | how often another may undo what one holds before giving up        | resolve in the arm   |

  The fiction ladder measured the range (rung 2, the Gerundelle arm):
  - Impatient Mara: tries the flag and the lid, never the latch she was curious about, gives up.
  - Restless Mara: never asks Ben, opens the box, learns nothing ("learns many things shallowly").
  - Dogged Mara: asks, tests the answer, is right in 26 sentences.
  - Skeptical Mara: tests the alternatives to Ben's word first.
  - Rung 4's endings flip on Leo's stubbornness: stubborn Leo outlasts Mara; default Leo gives up.

  On rung 2, all five lean profiles tell the same story: the lean mind's choices are decided by expected work and the want's doubling, so curiosity, persistence, and
  credulity have nothing to act on there. "Gerundelle is the wider substrate." The range of stories — different characters in the same scene making different choices for
  structural reasons — is wider in the richer mind.

  The corpus (fiction LADDER.md, corpus run 1): every scene × six temperaments × both minds: 120 runs. "Of six temperaments per scene, Gerundelle tells 3-6 different stories
  (median 4), the lean mind 1-5 (median 2)."

  Mapping to the Big Five:

  - Openness (receptivity to new experience): maps onto curiosity (Params.curiosity). A high-curiosity mind wanders, explores unclaimed regions, tries things no want pays
    for. A low-curiosity mind stays on the want.
  - Conscientiousness (persistence, goal-directed effort): maps onto persistence (Params.persistence) and patience (Params.patience). A persistent mind keeps investigating
    the same question. A patient mind keeps pursuing the same want through failures.
  - Agreeableness (cooperativeness, trust): maps onto credulity (Params.credulity). A credulous mind acts on what it's told at nearly full weight. A skeptical mind discounts
    told knowledge and tests it first.
  - Neuroticism (emotional reactivity, anxiety): maps onto the phase system's thresholds. Anxiety forms when a concept that matters has mostly bad outcomes and is understood
    below 0.4 (Phases.graph). A mind with a lower threshold for anxiety formation (or slower anxiety closure) would be more neurotic — but this is not yet a temperament
    parameter; the thresholds are global.
  - Extraversion: no direct mapping in the current system. The fiction ladder's social rungs (2-6) have minds that ask, tell, lie, and contest, but the propensity to initiate
    social interaction is not a parameter. A mind Poses (asks) when it holds a control slot with no way and someone is present — that's a structural condition, not a trait.

  How it differs from Big Five / trait theory specifically:

  - Big Five traits are statistical: extracted from factor analysis of self-report questionnaires across populations. Here, the traits are structural: each parameter has a
    specific computational meaning (the pull of unknowns, the decay rate of repeated experiments, the weight of told receipts) and a measurable effect on behavior (number of
    tries, which objects are explored, whether told knowledge is tested).
  - Big Five traits are orthogonal (by construction, the five factors are uncorrelated). Here, the parameters are independent but their effects interact: a patient,
    persistent, curious mind explores deeply; an impatient, persistent, curious mind starts many things and finishes none. The interactions are not prescribed — they fall
    out of the competition under scarcity.
  - Big Five says nothing about where traits come from. Here, the traits are parameters of the same algebra. "Different hyperparameters are different people, not different
    bugs" (IMPORTANT_REALIZATIONS.md). A mind with any combination of parameter values is a working mind — some more effective in some environments than others, none broken.
  - The fiction ladder's rung 7 showed that temperament determines narrative: the same scene, the same world, different hidden reserves produce four different endings.
    Personality is not color applied to a fixed story; it is the variable that determines which story is told.
