> Where does Kahneman's System 1 / System 2 fit into the system?

● Here's where it lands in the running system.

  Kahneman's dual-process model (Thinking, Fast and Slow, 2011): System 1 is fast, automatic, effortless — pattern recognition, intuition, the immediate answer. System 2 is
  slow, deliberate, effortful — conscious reasoning, working through a problem step by step.

  The learning curve in the concept map traces the transition explicitly: "A mind that knows nothing scans: every candidate tried, every failure uninformative (cognitivecode
  rung 0: 66 tries). A mind that was told a condition scans less: it knows what to look for (rung 2: 61 tries, 2 runs). A mind that recalls a shape barely searches at all:
  the past turns a blind scan into a guided handful (rung 5b: 3 tries). A mind with a fully cached convergence doesn't search: the resonance fires from storage in one tick
  (muscle memory). The cost of search is proportional to the mind's ignorance — the number of unfunded slots — not to the size of the solution space."

  System 1 is a cached constraint convergence: the mind has seen enough failures to fund every constraint, and the solution fires from storage. System 2 is the active
  tournament: molecules competing, experiments run, constraints being learned. They're not two systems. They're the same system at different points on the learning curve.

  The chess ladder's rung 1d measured the transition directly. In the first half, the mind learns constraints from failures (System 2: deliberate, each failure informative).
  In the second half, 87 of 100 new positions are solved at the first attempt (System 1: the constraints converge and the answer is noticed without search). The mechanism is
  candidate support in superposition (Params.support = "candidates"): each constraint learned from a different failure binds to the untried move that clears them all. Not
  searched for. Recognized. The measured numbers: first half mean 6.47 attempts, second half 1.82, 87 of 100 at the first attempt.

  How it differs from Kahneman specifically:

  - Kahneman's System 1 can't explain itself — it produces an answer but not a reason. Here, a cached convergence carries its receipts: "these five failures revealed these
    five constraints, and this solution is the only one that survives all of them." The fast answer IS the explanation. Understanding is inspectable (concept map: "Every slot
    has receipts. Every receipt has provenance. Every condition has the failures that revealed it").
  - Kahneman treats the two systems as separate architectures that sometimes conflict (the famous bat-and-ball problem: System 1 gives the wrong answer, System 2 corrects it).
    Here, they're one architecture at different stages of the same process. There's no conflict — only a transition from search to recognition as understanding grows. A wrong
    fast answer means the constraints were learned from too few failures (an over-general constraint from too coarse a perception: rung 1d finding 4, the queen mate at 36-37
    attempts because "a check on a guarded square is taken" was learned from cases where the queen was not backed).
  - Kahneman's framework has no account of how System 1 patterns are formed. Here, the formation is the ladder: each failure reveals a constraint, the constraints accumulate,
    and when enough of them converge on one candidate, the mind transitions from search to recognition. The formation is the learning. System 1 is what System 2 becomes.
