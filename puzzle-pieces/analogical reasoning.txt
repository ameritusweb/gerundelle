> Where does analogical reasoning (Gentner's structure-mapping theory) fit into the system?

● Here's where it lands in the running system.

  Structure-mapping theory (Gentner, 1983): analogy is about relations, not features. "The atom is like the solar system" maps because both have a central body and orbiting
  bodies with a force between them — the relations match, even though the features don't (the sun is hot, the nucleus is small). The mapping is structural: it preserves the
  relational structure (what is doing what to what) and drops the surface features (what things are made of, how big they are). Gentner calls this the "systematicity
  principle": a mapping that preserves higher-order relations (relations between relations) is preferred over one that preserves isolated matches.

  Recall by shape is the mechanism. The concept map: "Recall by shape: a condition learned in one domain, keyed by gerund structure not by names, transfers to a new domain
  where the structure matches. A past episode's shape is offered as a hypothesis at half weight, confirmed by the mind's own interaction."

  In the fiction ladder's rung 6, this is measured end to end:

  Scene A: Mara watches Ben open a box whose lid is held shut by a latch. The condition learned: the latch holds the lid shut. The episode is stored, keyed by structure:
  a push on an object in the gerund "fastened", with a condition about a connecting object in the same gerund.

  Scene B: Mara is alone with a cupboard whose door is held shut by a bolt. She has never seen this cupboard. The bolt is not the latch. The door is not the lid. No names
  match.

  The recall (SovAgent.recall, fiction LADDER.md rung 6): stuck on the wanted object with nothing held, the mind looks for a warm episode of the same shape — a push on an
  object in the same gerund (fastened), a condition whose object was in the same gerund (a connecting object that fastens). The latch episode matches. The condition is mapped
  onto this scene: latch → bolt. The recalled condition is held at half weight (a hypothesis to test, not a certainty). Her own tries confirm it.

  "Mara remembered how Ben had opened the box. She wondered what the bolt did. She pushed the bolt hard. The bolt gave way."

  The strip() function (in the concept map): "removes names from a gerund tree, leaving typed slots where names were. Two programs that do the same operations on the same
  shape of data flow land on the same skeleton, whatever their variable names, comments, or domain. A stone arch and a steel beam share one skeleton. 'The company moved to
  London' and 'the family emigrated to Canada' share one skeleton."

  The cognitivecode ladder's rung 7 showed composition by analogy: a header from one seed combined with a growth from another produced programs search never found. The
  generative power is combinatorial — a small set of gerunds and a nesting rule produce a space of structures large enough to cover both natural language and code.

  The Gerundelle arm's rung 6 was given a "fair test" (fiction LADDER.md): the bolt's id sorted last (so the mind didn't try it first by alphabetical accident). Result:
  48 sentences, reader satisfaction 0.668 (dogged: 0.686). "Mara remembered how Ben had opened the box." The analogy transferred, was tested, confirmed. Reader satisfaction
  beat the lean arm (0.643).

  How it differs from structure-mapping theory specifically:

  - Gentner's theory maps between two explicitly presented representations (the base and the target are both given). Here, the base is retrieved from storage by shape match
    — the mind is not told "this is like the box", it finds the match because the stored episode's gerund structure overlaps with the current situation's. Retrieval and
    mapping are one process, not two.
  - Gentner's mapping preserves relational structure and drops features. Here, the mechanism is more specific: strip() removes names and leaves typed slots. The "relations"
    that are preserved are the gerund nesting and the force-slot types. Two structures match if their stripped skeletons are the same — the same doings in the same nesting,
    whatever the objects are called.
  - Gentner's theory doesn't say what weight to give the transferred knowledge. Here, the recalled condition is held at half weight — a hypothesis, not a fact. The mind must
    test it in the new domain. If the world confirms it, the half-weight receipt is joined by a lived one. If not, it's retracted. Analogy produces a guess, not understanding.
  - The Gerundelle arm's recall (SovAgent.recall) explicitly tests this: "Gerundelle's analogy layer transfers that a relation is expected, not what fills it; the past did
    not help (14 tries against 11 fresh)" — in the fair test, the analogy tells the mind that some connecting object matters, but not which one. The help comes from narrowing
    the search, not from giving the answer.
  - Gentner's systematicity principle (prefer mappings that preserve higher-order relations) is structural here: the gerund skeleton IS the higher-order relations. There is no
    separate preference for systematicity — the skeleton is all that survives stripping, and the skeleton is relational by construction (gerunds with typed force slots).
